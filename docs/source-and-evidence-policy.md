# Source and Evidence Policy

## Current-Event Rule

For current events, refresh sources at the start of each debate run. Add the date, time zone, and source-access date to the brief.

Do not assume yesterday's facts are still true in an active conflict.

## Evidence Tiers

Tier A: Primary and technical sources

- Government releases, congressional records, official transcripts, IAEA/UN documents, military briefings, court records, UKMTO maritime reports, direct treaty or legal texts.

Tier B: High-reliability straight news

- AP, Reuters, BBC, AFP, Financial Times straight-news reporting, and similar outlets with editorial standards and corrections processes.

Tier C: Reported analysis and expert interpretation

- Major newspapers, specialist publications, think tanks, academic experts, energy-market analysis, regional-security analysis.

Tier D: Opinion and factional argument

- Politicians, campaign accounts, opinion columns, cable-news commentary, podcasts, comedy shows, influencers, social posts.

Tier D can show what a faction believes or argues. It cannot by itself verify a factual claim.

## Fact Ledger Labels

- Verified: supported by strong evidence from Tier A or multiple independent Tier B sources.
- Likely: supported by credible reporting, but still missing key primary confirmation.
- Contested: credible sources disagree, or official claims conflict with independent reporting.
- Unsupported: asserted without enough evidence.
- False: contradicted by stronger evidence.
- Superseded: was accurate earlier, but later events changed it.

## Claim Handling

Every major claim should be rewritten as a testable proposition:

- Bad: "Trump made the world safer."
- Better: "The operation reduced Iran's ability to threaten regional shipping for at least six months."

Every causal claim should name the mechanism:

- What changed?
- Who changed it?
- Compared with what alternative?
- How would we know?

## Citation Standards

Use links and dates for all factual claims. Quote sparingly. Prefer paraphrase plus citation. Mark a source's viewpoint when it is partisan, official, advocacy, or opinion.

## Arbiter Corrections

When the arbiter flags a claim, the agent must do one of four things:

- Provide stronger evidence.
- Narrow the claim.
- Reclassify it as opinion or prediction.
- Retract it.
