Evidence frame Before the agents answer

Why the claim is hard to parse

The frame starts with a hard technical number: Iran had hundreds of kilograms of uranium enriched up to 60% U-235, far beyond ordinary reactor fuel. The debate is whether that meant a near-term bomb, a severe breakout risk, or a political claim that outran the public evidence.

What the uranium number means Iran had a large stockpile of uranium enriched to a level that raises breakout-risk alarms, but enriched uranium alone is not a finished nuclear weapon.
1
Most reactor fuel is around 3-5% enriched.

That is the normal civilian-energy reference point people mean when they talk about nuclear power fuel.

2
Iran's reported 60% stockpile is far beyond that.

Once material is enriched that high, getting closer to weapons-grade takes much less additional enrichment work.

3
But fuel material is not the same as a bomb.

The debate is whether this was a severe breakout risk, proof of a near-term weapon, or a political claim that went beyond public evidence.
